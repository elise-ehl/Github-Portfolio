Arduino line follower

2025 · EEEE1031, Applied Mechatronic Construction Project

The goal was to build an autonomous 4WD Arduino line-following vehicle. The project covered PID control theory, sensor implementation and adaptable navigation across a series of weekly challenges, using trial and error, critical thinking and applied knowledge.

My role: software development and debugging with AI-assisted coding tools, PID testing and tuning, and project management.

Assessment: weekly challenge performance, plus oral and written reports.

View the code on GitHub

Weekly challenges

WeekChallengeHardwareResult
1 Drive straight for 10 s and show elapsed time on the LCD. DC motors and L298N motor driver Completed
2 Follow a track with 90° turns and curves in under 60 s, and display total distance. IR sensors to detect the track, encoders to measure distance Total distance: 596 cm
3 Track challenge: stop at 10 cm for 2 s, ascend a ramp, display the ramp angle on the LCD, pause for 4 s and rotate 360°. Other challenges: obstacle avoidance and Bluetooth control. MPU6050 for orientation on the ramp, HC-SR04 for obstacle avoidance, HC-05 for Bluetooth control Completed

Problems and how I solved them

  • Pin management. The motor driver and LCD shield competed for the same pins, so I re-planned the pin layout several times.
  • Track environment. Sun glare, and a test track that differed slightly from the real one, confused the IR sensors. I debugged the code repeatedly until the car worked under those conditions.
  • Overshooting curves. The car kept overshooting curves, which I fixed by tuning the PID values carefully.

Pit-stop challenge: pneumatic piston

2026 · MMME1035, Engineering Design and Design Project

The Pit-Stop Challenge asks teams to build machines that place a 50 mm foam block on a 75 × 75 mm area on a remote-controlled vehicle. It is based on Formula One pit stops. The project ran over a semester and included machining.

Challenge requirements

The pit stop runs in order. The vehicle enters the designated area, and Group 1 places a pit board in front of it to show where to stop. Group 2 then places the foam block on the vehicle, and Group 3 helps Group 2 extract their device. As Group 3, our job was to design and fabricate a piston that holds the foam block down while Group 2 retracts their piston.

Design specifications

A V-shaped attachment on the piston proved stable for holding the foam block down. I determined the piston size and stroke by calculation, based on the distance from the test rig to the foam block’s position on the vehicle.

Manufacturing and machining

I machined the pneumatic piston components: front end cap, piston body, tie rods, piston attachment, piston blank and others. The operations included marking and scribing, cutting, drilling on a pillar drill, and threading with a die. Materials were aluminium and bright drawn mild steel.
